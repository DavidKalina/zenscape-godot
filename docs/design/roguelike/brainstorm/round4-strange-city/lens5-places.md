# Round 4, lens 5: non-Olmec places for the city

Seven places an underground Mesoamerican city would have that are not the
Olmec's body: a well, a mercury court, a bath house, a mine, a painted tomb,
a drowned garden, a rubber grove. Each is a place first (the arrival view),
then one inhabitant or trap with one strange rule. Numbers use the wanderer's
real moves (`pilot_motor.gd`: WALK 2.4, RUN 4.8, wall-run, wall-kick, twirl,
slide) and the light API (`SightLight`, `Beacon`, `LightLevel`, the torch
node) from `beacons-light-sight-round1.md`. Kit names are guesses to verify
at greybox; nothing here was run.

---

## p1-ripple-well: The Ripple Well — "They came to the water to be quiet. The water remembers every step."

Pitch: You come out on a lip of limestone over a round sinkhole, a hundred
metres across, and a single pale column of far-off daylight hangs down the
middle onto black water. Round the water stand a dozen white figures, knee
deep, heads bowed, bowls in their hands, perfectly still, and not one ring
of ripples on the whole surface.

Place: A cenote: a collapsed vault, sheer calcite walls streaked green, a
shelf-ring of drowned steps going down to the water and a broken processional
stair coming up the far side. The ruin is the well-house: a colonnade that
ringed the rim and fell inward, its columns standing in the water like a
second row of mourners. Stone is calcite-crusted basalt, wet-dark below the
waterline, white and flaking above. Roots hang down the daylight shaft.
Arrival: from the north lip, daylight column dead centre, the figures as
small pale marks round it, the far stair a thin line.

Top (1 char ~ 4 m):
```
        ^^^^ in          ^ rim in (N lip)
     ,,,|""""|,,,         o wader (standing)
   ,,,o~~~~~~~~o,,,       ~ black water, shallow ring / deep centre
  ,,o~~~~ ||| ~~~~o,,     ||| daylight column
  ,,o~~~~ ||| ~~~~o,,     : drowned steps (walk the ring)
   ,,,o~~~~~~~~o,,,       = fallen colonnade (stones in water)
     ,,,:::::==,,,        S far stair out
          S
```

Inhabitant: **the Waders**. Calcite-white bearers, taller than the wanderer,
knees locked, bowl held out at the chest. They walk the water ring on the
drowned steps without bending their legs, like a procession on rails.
Real material, wet at the hem, a green bloom at the shins.

The strange rule: **A Wader moves only toward a ripple, and only while the
ripple lasts.** Still water, still Wader. A splash (landing in the shallows,
a thrown stone, a falling pillar, a swat) starts rings, and every Wader
within earshot wades to the ring's centre, stops when the water settles. A
Wader that reaches you does not grab: it pours its bowl over you, which
soaks the torch (it goes out for a short while, and the Olmec's sight reads
you dark but you see nothing) and slicks the stone under you.
Tell: the bowl tips a hand before it pours; each Wader hums one low note
while moving. Escalation: after four ripples in a minute the Waders stop
stopping, wading until the next ripple anywhere, and the hum becomes a drone
a headless body hears.

Hooks: sound (the drone is a Beacon-like noise lure), light (the pour
douses the torch), edges (the ring steps are narrow, the wet slick), breakables
(fallen colonnade stones are stepping stones; a Wader walking through one
shoves it), the Olmec (a body wading in is rippling water itself).

Play:
- Poor/safe: walk the drowned steps at WALK speed, never run, never land
  hard; keep to the dry colonnade top (a long wall-top route, 90 s). Nothing
  moves. One twirl-landing in the shallows and the line of Waders turns to
  you.
- Clever/rich: the rare sits in a Wader's bowl, centre-ring. Toss a pillar
  chip or drop from a high step into the far shallows to pull the whole
  ring across the well away from the stair, then run the near steps to the
  bowl. Better: lead them all onto the far side, then let a headless body
  come in off the north lip; his steps are a continuous ripple and he draws
  every Wader onto him, which jams the ring.
Rule 0: a ripple is a lure you can place; the Waders are a slow, soft,
repeatable decoy, and a crowd of them walling off the Olmec's path is a gate.
Risk: the soaked torch (you can't see the steps in the dark), and the ring
steps are slick when wet, with water past them.
Co-op: one friend is the splash, leaping off the far colonnade, while the
other works the bowl. The splasher then has to keep still on a column top
with the drone rising: whoever twitches decides who's next.

Generator fit: hero or set-piece; footprint ~140 x 140 m, height span -20
to +30 (the lip above the water, a stair out). Once per layer at most; a
quarter of layers.
Build cost: M. A pool disc with a ring, 10-12 reuse-able figure models on
rails with a hum and a bowl; ripples are an event (landing impact in
water) with a radius.
Cliché check: the stock "water level" or fish-in-water level; here the
water is a sound surface, not a hazard. The Wader is not Weeping Angels
(it needs no line of sight), it is a noise-led decoy.

## p2-quicksilver-court: The Quicksilver Court — "A pool you cannot drink, built to be crossed by something."

Pitch: A courtyard the size of a field is floored by one silver mirror, the
whole undercity upside down in it, the haze and your own hat. On its far
shore a dark boat of stone, bowl-shaped, sits waiting, and at the near shore
a dead cresset.

Place: A cut basin lined in cinnabar-stained basalt (dull ochre-red, never a
glow) holding a lake of liquid mercury: heavy, flat, with a skin that wobbles
and rolls into beads on anything stepped in. Stone floats on it, so fallen
columns drift as rafts and the colonnade that once rimmed the pool lies
half-afloat. Wanderer cannot stand in it (it is a sink/slide, not a swim).
Arrival: a processional on the near shore, a ruler-straight causeway
disappearing into the mirror, broken at the middle where a pillar rafted off.

Top:
```
  [near shore]  C dead cresset      F ferry
   C==== ,'''''''''''''''''.        = broken causeway
        ,.  silver mirror  .,       r rafts (drifting columns)
        ,.   r        r   F .,      L lit cresset on the far shore
         '..............L'
              [far shore: gate out]
```

Inhabitant: **the Ferryman**: a hollow cast of basalt in the shape of a
bowed, hooded figure kneeling in a canoe, no face, its paddle fused to its
hands. It glides on the mercury without a sound and carries one or two
standing passengers.

The strange rule: **the Ferryman always crosses toward the brightest fire on
the shore, and does not care who is aboard.** Both shores have a cresset;
whichever is lit and brighter is where it goes, and it goes at once, and
turns round when the other one is brighter. It will not leave while no
cresset is lit.
Tell: its hood turns a full second toward the flame before it moves; the
mirror carries the flame's reflection to it in a line across the pool.
Escalation: if the fire dies mid-crossing, it stops dead on the open silver
and turns slowly (a minute) before choosing again. A beacon tower near the
court draws the Olmec to the pool's lip, and the Ferryman crosses toward that
fire too.

Hooks: light/torch (your torch counts as a fire only when no cresset is lit),
the Olmec (his beacons are fires), edges (a stone raft is a platform with no
rim, mercury drinks anything fallen), breakables (rafts are fallen pillars
that still float).

Play:
- Poor/safe: skip the pool by the rim ledge, narrow and broken, wall-kick
  across three gaps, 2 minutes, nothing to gain.
- Clever/rich: light the near cresset, step aboard, light the far cresset
  from the boat? You can't: light the far one first by torch-hopping a raft
  (a dangerous twenty metres of drifting stone), come back, board, and ride.
  The rare sits on the pool's mid islet, reachable from the boat if you
  time the turnaround. Dousing the near cresset behind you, so it can't
  call the boat back, is optional and gives a free passage.
Rule 0: the ferry is a lift on a fuse: set the fire, set the destination.
Risk: a pool crossing is a straight open sightline; a lit cresset on the far
shore calls the Olmec; the ferry arrives where the fire is, so a body can
already be waiting.
Co-op: one friend goes ahead to the far cresset and lights it, the other
rides. Two lit cressets, one Ferryman: whoever lights last decides, and a
shout of "dont light yours" is the whole story.

Generator fit: hero; footprint ~160 x 120 m, height ~10; one per layer at
most, in the middle third.
Build cost: M. A flat pool, a ferry on a slow path with a target chooser
(Beacon-like pull on cressets), a few floating rafts reusing the drift stones.
Cliché check: the stock "moving platform" and "ferryman" are both here, so
the rule is the thing: the boat's brain is a brightness comparator the player
can set, not a route.

## p3-sweat-house: The Sweat-House — "A room so hot the old ones left their eyes at the door."

Pitch: Beyond a low door you step into a warm drowned fog, and a ring of
round-roofed bath-chambers glows dull amber out of it, steam leaking from
every seam. You hear a ladle before you see who holds it.

Place: A temazcal cluster, six beehive domes of fitted lava-stone around a
hot spring, stepped into a hillside; the domes broke at the crowns and
their stoves (fire pits, ash-grey) still smoulder. A flagged yard in the
middle is slick with condensation. Material: soot-dark basalt, green sweat
on the walls, bright mineral crust round the vents. Arrival: from the high
door the whole cluster lies below like a cluster of eggs in a nest, steam
combing across them.

Top:
```
      ^ door in       D  domes (sealed rooms, cover)
   ##########        ~  yard of steam
   # D  D   #        v  vents (updraft)
   # ~~~~~v #        A  the Attendant
   # D A  D #       -> out lower right
   ##########
```

Inhabitant: **the Attendant**: a stooped stone bather-keeper, a ladle and
bucket, arms too long, back worn smooth. He walks between the domes pouring
water on the hot stones; each pour is a gout of steam and a hot updraft
through the dome's vent.

The strange rule: **He pours for the slow and stops for the fast.** While
anything moves at WALK pace or less in his yard he keeps pouring; at RUN
speed (or a hard landing) he stops, turns, and the steam falls in seconds.
Steam hides: the Olmec's sight range inside it is a few metres, and your
torch is swallowed. It also hides the edge.
Tell: the ladle rhythm and a long hiss; the steam bank is the readout.
Escalation: if you keep him pouring for a minute the stones go slick
(skid threshold drops) and a vent that has been poured on four times bursts
(a launch, up and sideways).

Hooks: light (steam kills your torch's range and sight), sound (a run stops
him), edges (the yard has no rim wall), the Olmec (an Olmec walking in steam
blinds itself, so it stands and listens, and hears a run).

Play:
- Poor/safe: skirt the cluster along the high wall, lit and seen, 1 minute,
  past a body that patrols the door.
- Clever/rich: walk the yard at 2.4 under cover of steam, domes to dome,
  reach the central vent, wait for a pour, and glide the burst (twirl then
  pocket float) up to the roof of the tallest dome where the rare sits.
  The Olmec in the yard, blind, can be herded: run once at the far end, he
  hears and goes.
Rule 0: the Attendant is a smoke screen on your walking speed. Risk: the
steam also hides the edge and the Olmec stands inside it.
Co-op: the walker stays slow and quiet; the other runs one loud lap round
the outside to pull the sound off them. Cruelty and grace in one room.

Generator fit: set-piece; footprint ~100 x 100 m, height ~12 in a bowl.
Frequency: 1 in 3 layers.
Build cost: M. A steam volume that gates sight and torch range (LightLevel
already reads exposure), one patrol keeper reacting to player speed, a
vent launch.
Cliché check: the stock fog/stealth level and fire-and-ice fog hazards.
Rule here is a pacing rule on one inhabitant, and the sound is speed itself.

## p4-green-seam: The Green Seam — "Everything they carved here, they carved from under their own feet."

Pitch: A gallery of scaffolds hangs down a cleft in the rock, every stage
lined with raw green: a seam of jade, striped and glassy, running down the
wall as far as the eye goes. Stone men stand along the way with their
shoulders under the roof.

Place: A vertical quarry-mine, a split in basalt with timber-and-stone
catwalks, ladders cut into the face and a lift-basket on a broken rope. The
jade seam glints in strata; chunks have been pried away and left in
carts. Material: dust-pale walls, glassy green cuts, rope-polished stone.
Arrival: from the top rim, a fall of the whole mine below you, the seam
a green vein you could follow with a finger.

Side:
```
 rim in ===========
   S    S         S   S shorer
   ===J=====       J jade (collectible)
     S   S==J==
  ====J    S
       ====== out
```

Inhabitant: **the Shorers**: heavy caryatid figures, each stooped and
braced under a roof slab, a hand on the next prop. They hold the gallery up
and do not move.

The strange rule: **A Shorer puts down its prop and walks to whoever
took jade from its section.** Taking a jade chunk from the seam breaks a
tie: the nearest Shorer lets go, the slab above sags, and it walks
(slowly, stooped) toward the thief to take it back; if it arrives, it
hits like a closing door. If it leaves its place, the slab it held falls.
Tell: before it moves, grit trickles from its slab and the figure
straightens by a hand; its prop lowers a hair a second.
Escalation: each additional chunk frees another Shorer; at three the gallery
begins to lean and everything on it slides toward the cleft.

Hooks: greed (the jade is the rare and the runes), breakables (falling slabs
are rubble to climb), edges (the catwalks sag toward the drop), the Olmec
(a falling slab on his head is the best weapon in the game), carrying (the
Shorer wants the jade back; the pickup is the lure).

Play:
- Poor/safe: take the ladder on the far wall, skip the seam; nothing falls,
  nothing is gained.
- Clever/rich: take one jade from a Shorer near an Olmec's patrol, walk it
  along the catwalk so the Shorer follows over the Olmec, then drop through
  a gap as its slab comes down. The slab crushes (or stuns) the body and
  becomes a bridge over the gap.
Rule 0: freed Shorers are a slow, readable, controllable walking mass: aim
them. Risk: three chunks and the mine is on the move, and a Shorer's blow
knocks you into the cleft.
Co-op: one friend takes the green, the other stands under the slab that
matters. The friend whose job is to stay still is the friend who dies if
the Shorer is late.

Generator fit: set-piece; footprint ~80 x 80 m footprint, a 60 m drop (the
tallest place in the list). Frequency: 1 per run.
Build cost: L. Catwalk kit, 4-6 figures with a leave-post-and-walk state, a
breaking slab, ladders; the figures are statue-rigged.
Cliché check: the stock "take the treasure, the cave collapses" trap. The
collapse here is not a timer, it is a creature, and you steer it.

## p5-walking-frieze: The Walking Frieze — "Read the wall before you lean on it."

Pitch: You step into a long barrel-vaulted tomb whose walls are painted from
floor to vault in ochre, white and a rust-red the colour of old pigment,
one huge procession in relief, every figure walking the same way. You
notice, a minute in, that the nearest one has moved.

Place: A cinnabar-sealed tomb, a long chamber dug under a platform, its
inner room a burial pit with a stone sarcophagus under a lid. The frieze
runs round the whole gallery, figures a metre tall, bearers, drummers, a
captive, a lord. Stone: lime-plastered basalt with pigment flaking and a
dust of red at the base. Arrival: from the doorway the room runs away
into dim, the frieze a long colour-line at hand height, one pale bowl
in the sarcophagus room visible at the far end.

Top:
```
  [door]===============================
   | > > > > > > > >  the frieze moves > > |
   |                                        |
   | floor: low ledges and pit steps         |
   | < < < < < < < <   (opposite wall)       |
   ================================[tomb]
```

Inhabitant: **the Frieze**: not a figure but a procession, painted bearers
that are alive in the plaster. They walk along the wall at a steady
pace, loop at the corners, and lap the room every ninety seconds.

The strange rule: **They only touch what touches the wall.** A hand, a
boot, a wall-kick, a wall-run. A figure passing under your hand shoves you
off the wall, with a push on the walk's direction. The floor and the air are
safe. Tell: the plaster in their path lifts a fraction and a thin red dust
sifts, a tell a stride ahead of each figure; a drummer figure makes a soft
beat the player learns as a clock.
Escalation: break the plaster in their way (slam) and the gap is a stop;
the figures bunch and the next lap carries a bearer with the burial bowl
lowered. Break the lord and the whole frieze reverses.

Hooks: edges (wall-runs near the pit, a shove puts you in it), breakables
(plaster slabs), sound (the beat), light (the torch makes figures face you,
a lord looks at the light and stops).

Play:
- Poor/safe: walk the floor, a long ledge route with no wall use, 90 s,
  but every vertical gap in the tomb has to be passed by the wall.
- Clever/rich: wall-run the lap exactly with the drummer, riding behind a
  figure's shoulders (it never touches the wall behind itself), up to the
  vault to the high bowl; or hold the wall *with* a bearer: wall-slide
  alongside its walking, and the push carries you on.
Rule 0: the shove is a launch if aimed: a wall-kick into a figure's
stride sends you sideways with its speed. Risk: the pit below and the
timing is a metronome you must not lose.
Co-op: one friend reads the beat aloud, one wall-runs; the friend
standing in the doorway is the one who sees the lap closing.

Generator fit: set-piece (interior); footprint ~60 x 30 m, a 12 m high
vault, one per run.
Build cost: M. A scrolling relief shader with hit windows is cheap; the
wall-run shove is a new `knock` source; no skeletons.
Cliché check: the stock "wall of spikes that moves". Here the wall is a
timetable you read, and its danger is only where you grip it.

## p6-drowned-milpa: The Drowned Milpa — "Someone is still weeding. Be shorter than the corn."

Pitch: Beyond a black lake, a mile of floating garden beds in long straight
rows, each bank thick with pale corn grown tall in the dark, roots hanging in
the water, one lamp lit at the end of every row. Something stooped is walking
the third row.

Place: A chinampa grid: narrow raised beds on a still lake, divided by
canals, willow-like trees staked at the corners that now root into the
ceiling. The beds are rich soil and stone-edged. Corn here is pale,
overgrown, as tall as the wanderer in places. Material: wet earth, pale
stalks, mossed stone borders. Arrival: from a high causeway, the grid laid
out like a ruled page, lamps in rows, long pale shapes, the canals as
black slashes.

Top:
```
    ====== causeway (in)
   |~~|""""|~~|"""""|~~|     "" corn beds     ~~ canal
   |~~|""W"|~~|""""o|~~|     W the Weeder    o lamp
   |~~|""""|~~|"""""|~~|
    ~~~ ~~~ ~~~ ~~~ ~~~
    out: far causeway
```

Inhabitant: **the Weeder**: a blind stone figure bent double at the hip,
fingers like trowels, shuffling along a bed.

The strange rule: **The Weeder pulls up the tallest thing in its bed.** It
stands at the end of a row and faces the highest point in front of it: a
stalk, a statue, you standing on a mound, an Olmec. It walks to it and
uproots it. Corn grows toward your torch (a bed lit for a minute shoots up a
body-length), so the torch plants the target. Crouch or stay low and it
ignores you.
Tell: its head tilts up to the highest point and a hand opens; you can see
exactly what it has chosen.
Escalation: after it uproots something it keeps a row for a minute, then
moves on a row; a second Weeder wakes on a fed bed.

Hooks: light (the torch grows corn, the lamp rows are Beacons), the Olmec (an
upright body is the tallest thing; a Weeder will go for him), edges (beds
are narrow and the canals are the drop), breakables (stalks).

Play:
- Poor/safe: crouch-crawl along the canal edge, under the corn, 3 minutes.
- Clever/rich: grow a stalk with the torch to climb to the lamp row's rare,
  but you are now the tallest thing in the bed. Jump off it as the Weeder
  arrives, so it uproots the stalk, and its hands are in the soil while you
  cross behind. Even better, lure an Olmec down the causeway beside the
  bed, so it is the tallest.
Rule 0: the Weeder is a pointing machine you aim with the tallest thing.
Risk: a grown stalk is a ladder with a countdown, and the Weeder is quick
over a flat bed.
Co-op: one friend grows the ladder with a held torch while the other
stands low and still as bait; the climber has to come down before the
Weeder gets there.

Generator fit: set-piece; footprint ~160 x 120 m, flat (height span 15).
Frequency: 1 in 3 layers; best in a layer's second half.
Build cost: M-L. Grow-by-torch plants and a "tallest in the row" chooser
are both simple, but the grid and the growth need an art pass.
Cliché check: the stock "plant monster" and "living garden" levels. Here
nothing attacks you for being there, only for being tall.

## p7-rubber-grove: The Rubber Grove — "The ball is a tree's blood, and the tree keeps what it is given."

Pitch: A grove of fat, scarred trees rises through a ceiling hole, bark cut in
neat diagonals, white latex beading in the cuts and pooling in cups at their
feet. The ground has a give to it you notice at the first step.

Place: A latex grove: old tapped trees on a spongy mat of cured rubber
over rock, the cups left under every cut, a ballcourt-wall ruin leaning in
the middle for a stand to look from. The floor gives at each step and
rebounds. Material: dark bark, white and amber latex, pale basalt courts.
Arrival: from the dry ridge, a stand of trunks like pillars, their
canopy dark with leaves, light stippling down.

Top:
```
    ridge in
     T  T  T       T tapped tree
   T   (c)  T  T   c latex cup (pool, a launcher)
     T  W   T      W the Wall of the old court
   T   T   T  T    exit through the far trunks
```

Inhabitant: **the trees themselves, the Rebounders**: each trunk returns the
last blow it took. Strike a tree (a slam, a shoulder-run, a hit from a
headless body) and, three seconds later, it gives that blow back, aimed at
whatever is nearest.

The strange rule: **A tree gives back the last thing that hit it, after a
delay of three seconds.** The blow is a knock from the trunk at the same
strength and in the direction of the nearest body.
Tell: a deep creak as it stores the hit and a slow settle in the bark as it
lets go. Escalation: three stored hits in one tree and it throws all at
once.

Hooks: physics/knock (trees are bouncers), the Olmec (his swat into a trunk
becomes a return swat at the next body, which can be himself), edges
(a rebound near a rim), breakables (latex cups).

Play:
- Poor/safe: walk the grove edge, avoid trees, 1 minute.
- Clever/rich: bait an Olmec into swatting a tree and stand so the nearest body is
  not you; or hit one yourself and use the rebound to launch you onto the
  court wall (the rare behind the wall).
Rule 0: a rebound is a delayed launch, aimed at the nearest body, so
you place yourself. Risk: the nearest body might be your friend.
Co-op: a friend shoves a tree, then the other has to be the nearest body.

Generator fit: filler or connector; footprint ~100 x 100 m; height ~8.
Build cost: S-M. A static trunk with a stored-hit state and a delayed
`knock`.
Cliché check: bouncy mushrooms and trampolines. The delay and the aim are the
thing; this is the weakest card (a launcher on a timer) and could be cut.

## Cut during drafting

- **Observatory with a noon beam**: a timed daylight lance that dazzles
  whatever stands in it was a place without an inhabitant, and the kneelers
  were the Olmec body again.
- **Cinnabar glow tomb**: red light is edgy; the frieze card took the tomb
  with dull pigment instead.
- **Ballcourt**: taken by the ghost-team seed in the brief.
- **Mercury "silver skin" blob enemy**: stock slime; the ferryman took the pool.
- **Weighing well** (ledges sink with the runes you carry): a copy of the
  Tithe-Taker's greed rule with no creature.
- **Stalactites that lean toward warmth**: a falling-ceiling trap, stock.
- **Swifts in the cenote shaft**: a flock, stock.
