# The Slumped Causeway — "They built it level. The ledger records how far it has come down."
Lenses: verb fall/descend (mirror: the long climb home) · shape a tall stack, one long diagonal · decay half-collapsed mid-fall · scale colossal · mood oppressive
Pitch: You step out of the high city onto a great causeway and it is not level. Ahead it runs out, slumped down into the haze in a long staircase of dropped spans, each one caught on the broken back of the one below, the far isle a lit speck 130 m away and 40 m under your boots. Olmecs stand on the tiers above and behind you, and they do not take the stairs.

Place: A causeway of the makers (the 26 m `city_causeway` spans, 11 m wide: an Olmec's lane), built once to link a high crown to a low one. A fault in the floor of the abyss let the far piers settle, and the whole line came down in order, span by span, like a dropped chain. You see it whole from the near shore: a kinked, stepped grey line going away and down, broken spans lying at 10 to 20 degrees, each lower end resting on the next span's upper end (a lap of 3 to 5 m, shoved, ridged and cracked), every pier below it either stub-snapped or leaning. The silhouette is a descending zigzag against a haze that gets browner with depth; the far isle (a pillar crown with six cressets, the next place) is small, low and warm. Where each span snapped it hinged at one end and sheared at the other: the sheared ends are ragged corbel-and-rubble faces with the rubble fan spilled onto the span below, so the collapse reads as sequence (this fell first, this one dragged by it) and not as plates. Two spans are missing entirely and the gap is the real jump. Growth hangs from the underside only (roots, pale moss, down-curtains); the tops are bare, swept dust and dropped masonry. Beneath, a cut-away: one pier's hollow corbel chamber, open to the dark, with a cresset lit in it long ago.

Play:
- Route A, the run down (about 100 s). Span 1 is whole and level: you run, the rest of the causeway hidden by its own kink. At the kink the deck ends in a clean hinge-break, and the next span sits 4.5 m lower, a leaning ramp. You drop onto the ramp (under the 6 m fall stun) and a slide takes you down its length, the pilot's slide carrying onto the lap where the ridge of the sheared end throws a hop. Each span repeats the idea with a different broken end: a ramp, a stepped fault, a span lying on edge with its curb as a rail slide. Beat 4 is a missing span: a 14 m gap with a 9 m drop. A plain long jump (about 9 m at a 4 m drop; `crossing-viaduct-round1`) does not make it; the pocket sail does. Lose it and you land on the lower span's far lap, with a fall stun (the 6 m threshold) and an Olmec arriving.
- Route B, the underside. Where each span has slid, the hollow beneath is a ledge chain: the pier's corbel courses (`maker_corbel`) are 1.6 m ledges (SET_OFF) you grab and hang-drop from, going down on the shaded side the Olmecs cannot see into. Slow, silent, many 5 m hang-drops, and the underside moss is slippery-feeling to look at but holds. Cost: you are on the stonework the dropping Olmec lands on.
- Route C, the express. Ride a dropped span's loose rubble fan with a flow-charge downdraft slam to cut two spans in one go (about 10 m straight down), landing on the third's back. Fast, the only place the slam is the right answer and not a stunt.
- Mirror (the way home, or a greed branch back up): the same line climbed. The spans are ramps, the pilot climbs by the sheared ends' corbel steps (2 m shelves, chain jumps) and the underside, and the Olmecs now come down onto you from above.
Threat: Headless bodies on the upper spans and the crown. They hunt by hearing (body_hearing) and the span's slope is the rule of the place: an Olmec does not walk a 15 degree ramp that ends at a 4.5 m drop, he sees you below and leaps off after you (a body is 4.4 m wide, so his landing shocks `SHOCK_HEARD` on the span, the lap rattles, and loose masonry from the sheared end slides). He lands where you were. His landing is also a danger to him: a body that jumps down onto a lap that is cracked can break it (see `maker_chock` and `colossus_plug`: the breakable lap is the city's own model). The trick is the pilot's: a span-end he drops onto turns into a ramp that carries him under the one above. The Olmec chain drops from span to span behind you, like boulders, one after another, never faster than the player can run, never the whole way: the lowest ones give up where the haze is and turn back (the `BAIL`).
Could combo: A pilot knocked by a landing Olmec onto a ramp slides into the lap fan and is flung by its ridge (a `toss` off the sheared end) onto the span below: a 5 m drop he can twirl-recover. An inhaler head hovering in the gap (see ports) pulls a pilot mid-sail back toward the upper span's edge, where the dropping Olmec's landing is. A rubble fan broken by a dropped Olmec raises the lower span's lap by 1 to 2 m and shortens the next ledge chain. Ports: catches a thrown or knocked body from the crown (lands on span 2 at about 6 to 8 m, stunned), and throws out onto the far isle's crown (the last span's lap is a slide ramp ending on the plaza).
Mystery: The makers did not build a level bridge. Every span is cut with its far end 2 m lower than its near one: a deliberate fall, a slope for rolling something heavy down in order. The last span has a stone cradle on it, empty. The tablets on the first pier count spans down, and one number is missing from the sequence: the gap. Something was lowered along this, and the fault did not cause that.

Family knobs: `drop` (30 to 55 m total; sets spans 5 to 9), `gap_count` (1 to 3 missing spans, each wider with depth), `lean` (how tilted, steeper = more slide, less walk), `host` (how many upper-span Olmecs, 1 to 3).
Sockets: rims at both ends (a crown shore each; high rim facing the descent, low rim facing the haze up); arenas on the middle laps (11 by 6 to 10 m, flat where two spans overlap); cover under the laps (the hollow chamber, out of sight); perches at every upper span's far corner for the Olmec to drop from.
Generator fit: a crossing motif with its own two shores, a long diagonal edge (about 130 m along, 40 m down); a good fit as an edge-shaped place between a high and a low neighbourhood in the city layer (`CITY_HOOD`), replacing the level `city_causeway` edge. Layer shape: a tall stack stretched sideways. The generator only moves isles, so the descent direction is by placement; the mirror climb is the same place entered from the low shore.
Distinct from: `serpent_crossing` (a level crossing on a living body) and `felled_colonnade` (a level fallen column row, the destruction round); this one is a descent where the break is the geometry, not a scripted event. Not the Broken Viaduct either, which is a level skill crossing with a single gap and no threat from above.
