# The Underhang — "Nothing walks the road. Everything hangs from it."
Lenses: Verb = time a crossing, stay hidden; Scale = colossal; Decay = intact and still working (the road is whole, its keepers are not); Mood = oppressive, silent; Shape = a line, a hanging underside; Twist = the threat is below (round 4 contrarian: the bridge's danger is under the span, and the bridge catches).

Pitch: A single great causeway, whole and handsome, runs 150 m across a haze-bottomed gulf, and nothing on it moves. Under it, bodies hang from the cross-beams like bats, and the only places they can reach you are the joints in the deck, one every few strides.

Place: The gulf is wider than anything else in the layer: the arrival isle is a squared, cut-stone forecourt high on the west rim with a dark gateway, the far isle a lone pier-crowned island 150 m east with a pale hall front and a lit cresset. Between them runs one causeway (city_causeway), twice the usual deck width, 5 m, laid in long basalt slabs on broad trestle piers; every 18 to 20 m the deck is cut by an expansion joint, an open slot 0.9 m wide and the whole width of the road, and the deck on either side is a couple of hand-widths different in height, so each joint is a step. The deck is bare, lined with low curbs, no ornaments, no ruin, nothing to climb on, nothing to hide behind: a floor of stone going out into fog, grave and clean, as if swept. From the arrival forecourt it reads as the quietest place in the game. It is only on the second look that you notice the underside of the road: between the trestle piers, hung in the shadow from the cross-beams by their hands, are the bodies, headless Olmecs clinging upside-down-ish, arms up on the beams, backs to the fog, three or four in the span, each below a joint. Once you see the shapes, the slots look like mouths. The stone: the weathered basalt of the Olmec, the deck rune-cut along its centre line in a single groove that runs the whole length like a lane marking. Fog builds from the far isle toward the middle so the middle third of the road is a pale deck on a pale ground.

Drop 6 m over 150 m: a gentle grade. The causeway's two ends are at the pilot's run, not jump, scale; the difficulty is the joints, and what is in each.

Play:
- Route A (the deck, the intended one). Straight along the deck. Each joint is 0.9 m, trivially jumped, but a joint is where the hanging body below can reach: its arm (headless_body arm 3.3 m; `swat_up` 2.5 m) comes up through the slot in a slow sweep, a grab that knocks the pilot sideways. You cross a joint on the beat after one arm retracts, or at a run, or sideways: the sweeping is slow (the body's own `swat_windup` 0.55) but wide. Four joints with bodies under them, every 18 m, evenly spaced, so the rhythm is walk, watch the slot, hop, walk, watch. Footfalls on the deck carry down: noise 4 m walking, 14 running; a run wakes the nearest; the walk does not. A pilot who sprints the whole span is chased to the far isle by three arms in turn, each handing him to the next joint. A pilot who walks it is never touched, and it takes a minute.
- Route B (the catch). Fall off the deck deliberately. Drop through a joint on the safe side (a joint with no body under it) onto the lower catwalk, a narrow service ledge hung from the trestles 5 m under the deck (GRAB_TOP lets him mantle back up at any pier; 5 m is a stun-free drop at about 20 m of landing noise, crouch the landing for x0.6). From the catwalk he can see the bodies from the side and go round them, hand over hand on the cross-beams (ledge grab and chain), safe from the arms since they reach up and he is level with them, but not from their hands on the beams; each body is a gate with a timed sweep. Quiet and in shadow, but the beams are narrow and the bodies' hands rest on them.
- Route C (breaking it, if the bridge is whole). The deck's slabs are a pair of long basalt slabs between joints. Slam a joint's end slab (a heavy landing from a long drop) and a body below is dislodged, or he knocks the slab onto the body: it falls, with the slab, into the haze. That makes the next joint's body twitch. A routed Olmec above, if the layer has a roaming headless body, will walk the same deck and its weight breaks nothing; this is a crossing where the Olmec on the deck and the Olmecs under it never agree.
Threat: The hanging bodies (headless_body with `cling` on a lip, scripts/enemies/headless_body.gd; the `cling` function holds one by its hands from a beam with `below` set to hang it under the deck). They are keepers of the road; they do not leave it. They hear the deck (the noise bus; a run is 14 m, a landing 5 + 6 a metre) and swing up through the joint under the loudest noise within reach. It is also a tool: the arm that comes up through a joint is a launch (`swat_out` 9 m, `swat_up` 2.5 m, still stunned) that can throw the pilot a joint or two ahead along the deck in a fast, controlled way: one hand-off, then the next body's turn. A tossed body that lands on a joint falls into the slot's 0.9 m only if it comes down on its side; usually it bounces on.
Could combo: the swat from joint 2 lands him on the lower catwalk's lip, stunned; the swat from joint 3 reaches up and drops him back on the deck, sleeping fast; the cresset on the far isle lights the end of the road and draws the head (`inhaler_head`) across the gulf in the clear air above the span, a hovering eye that sees everything on the deck while the bodies below do not: if it calls (the head's call is a beacon that the bodies answer), the whole span's keepers wake together. Ports: it catches thrown bodies on the deck (flat, rune-lined, a lane to slide along); it throws out through the far isle's gate (the forecourt side opens on a step).
Mystery: The causeway was the road of a procession that is not on it. The keepers hang beneath because the road was meant to be carried, held up by the makers' own bodies when the stone failed; the bodies are the road's supports now (they hang from the beams, bracing them), and the road would fall if they all let go. Keep this as the second look: the arms up on the beams are holding the deck.
