# Kit K5: the serpent's city (round 3 brainstorm, lens RIDE / CARRY)

**The family `kukul`** (a new file, say scripts/ruin_kit_kukul.gd, ids `kukul_*`). Kukulcan's work: what the city built for and around the feathered serpent, the rune serpent of docs/scenic/rune-serpent.md. Colossal, sacred, a shrine or a calendar. It builds on round 1's makers family (kit-k5-makers.md: CELL 4.4 m, SET_OFF tiers, the Olmec stone shader, `mounts`/`catches`/`throws`, `carom`). It does not repeat that family's tiers, palisades, thrones, stelae, courts or buried heads. The makers made the heads; the kukul family is the *road* the serpent keeps through their city.

## The idea in one paragraph

The serpent is the city's one moving thing. It never stops: the rune-serpent doc has it cruising at 12 m/s (8.5 to 17), 90 m long, 4 m through, on a closed B-spline lap that "stays inside the hull of its control points", with a slither of +-3.3 m. Down here its lap is shorter, a minute or two, and it is the city's **train and its lamp**. Every kukul piece is either a stop on its line, a thing it threads, or a thing built to trace its route in stone. The player learns the line from the stone before they ever see the serpent: balustrades curve along it, rings stand on it, a coil pillar winds up it. The wait for the ride happens on a wide dais with a shrine on it (a meaty place to be). The ride is a climb: its route spirals up between pillars.

## Shared rules

- **The line is data.** Every kukul piece carries `way` metadata: a list of `{p, tangent, bore}` control points in its own frame (the same shape as `RuneSerpent.ROUTE`, each point with weave/pace/veer shares). The motif concatenates the pieces' ways into one closed B-spline. Because a B-spline stays inside its control hull, the generator can check clearance from the points alone (the doc's own argument). Weave share is 0 inside a ring or a coil, so the body stays on the axis there, as it does in the pierced isle's arch.
- **The bore.** The tube the serpent needs is `RADIUS` 2 plus most weave 3.3 plus heave and veer margin, so **7 m radius (14 m opening)**, named `KUKUL_BORE` in the kit file. No plane exists down here, so this is not `WorldScale.opening()`; it is derived from the serpent's own constants (RADIUS, WEAVE x WAVE), never copied metres. Pilot-scale parts (rails, handholds, ledges) still come from the pilot (`SET_OFF` 1.6, `GRAB_TOP`, `CHAIN_TOP`, `STAND`, `TOP_ROOM`).
- **Olmec lanes.** Everything walked by the body is wide: stairs at least **6.6 m** (1.5 CELL, over his 5 m limit), landings and daises at least **8.8 x 8.8 m** (over 6x6), shelves are 6 m or more or under 2 m (never between). A break may take a ledge **below 2 m** or leave **6 m and more**, but never leave 2 to 6 m. Wherever a piece breaks, the broken state is checked against these in the card.
- **Riding is a new carry, one rule.** The serpent already has capsules on layer 1 moved each physics frame (13 of them, one per four rings plus the head). A ridden segment needs the pilot to **inherit the capsule's velocity while standing on it** (a moving-platform rule in PilotMotor). That is the only new physics the family asks for, and it is Opus work. While riding, the body is a long, slowly weaving, sloping floor 4 m wide: the crest and the belly make it a *rail* (rail slide) as much as a deck.
- **Sources.** `kukul_rail`, `kukul_dais`, `kukul_ring`, `kukul_coil`, `kukul_saddle`, `serpent_wake`. Each is its own source, so a chain through the kukul stone is not cut by `restun_gap` (1.2 s) against the headless body's shockwave, the inhaler head or a wind blaster.
- **Light, one rule.** The serpent carries its own light (runes, spine seam, eyes' haze, two ribbons of whiskers, the tail fin). Kukul pieces carry **runes that wake when it passes** and are dead stone otherwise: a rune strip, lit by distance to the nearest serpent capsule, with an ease of about 0.6 s. That is what lights the dark, and in haze it makes a moving **string of gold** across the isles that tells the player where the line goes. Dead, the strip is just grooves in the stone (the kit's rule that no ornament glows, kept). At most one dynamic light follows the serpent (the doc's "rune light" idea); the rest is emissive shader work, nearly free.
- **Destruction.** Every kukul piece breaks the way the colonnade does (olmec-destruction-round1.md): a scripted split into Jolt `RigidBody3D` pieces (a drum or a box per piece, friction 0.9, almost no bounce), a stump left, a hit-stop, dust, and rubble that lies where it fell. Walked floors and stair treads **never** break; what they carry does. So his lanes keep their width and the player's route is never destroyed by him. What he breaks makes **new, worse footing and new cover**.
- **Never hostile.** The serpent has no health, no damage, and is "never hostile". Its danger is **being a thing the size of a train in a room**. Wake shove (`serpent_wake`) is optional and physics-only.

# Feathered balustrade (kukul_balustrade, family kukul)
Verb: ride (rail slide down the serpent's back along a stair; the body of the stone serpent *is* the rail), climb (wall kick the cheek wall, ledge grab the head block), hide (behind the head block's lee), read (the way the serpent goes).
Look: the grand stair of the city, paired cheek walls rising its full length, each carved as a serpent of the same hand as the rune serpent: the body's crest a rail along the top, scales cut as faceted rings (the serpent's octagonal rings, a few percent out of true), gold-ready rune bands every third ring on the flank. At the foot, a colossal serpent head, mouth open, rests on the ground with its jaw as a step; at the head of the stair the tail lifts into a fin of stone feathers. The head is the same hand that carved the Olmec's helmet band and uses the Olmec stone shader. The stair between them is wide and plain: broad low steps in the makers' module (riser 0.4, tread 0.5, so four steps climb one tier). In weathered form the cheek walls are broken in places and the crest rail snaps; moss and strangler roots fill the broken coils; the head's eyes are dead sockets. The stair's run bends gently, as the balustrade always follows the line, so a great stair is always an S or a sweep, not a ladder.
Behaviour: static. Rune bands wake as the serpent passes: when the live serpent goes by (a stair that sits under its route or within 12 m), the runes on the nearest cheek flare in order, head to tail, as the pulse in its flank does. A *dead* stair (a cheek with no serpent on the line) stays dark. No moving part.
Size: stair width **6.6 m** clear between cheeks (1.5 CELL; over the Olmec's 5 m, with the cheeks as a separate 1.4 m wall each side, so the footprint is 9.4 m). Rise of one tier (SET_OFF 1.6) per four steps, up to eight flights with landings of at least 8.8 x 8.8 m (over his 6x6; the grand stair already has landings). The cheek wall is 2 SET_OFF (3.2) high at its low end, rising to 4 SET_OFF at the head; its crest is a rail 0.6 m wide, at the pilot's rail-slide height. The head block at the foot is a 3.2 m ledge (out of a standing grab, in the pump's reach: CHAIN_TOP 2.575 from a 0.8 m plinth of the jaw), and the crest meets it. A stair is 3 to 6 flights.
Collision: tread walk boxes (clean, the kit's `_walk_box`). The crest is a clean sloped-box rail (the same family of hull as the balustrade-slide the kit already has: "balustrade slide" is a foot piece in RuinKit). The cheek flank is a plumb box (wall kick). The head block's top is a walk box and the jaw is a ramp-shaped hull. The head's eye sockets are only shading.
Motifs it enables:
- **The Long Descent.** A grand stair that the player *rides down* as fast as they went up, rail slide down the left crest, with the serpent's line crossing the stair halfway; the gold string follows the rail, so the rail leads to the next stop.
- **The Procession Stair.** A stair that bends round a pillar so the serpent line at its head threads a ring (kukul_ring): wait on the stair, and the serpent comes down the opposite crest.
- **The Foot of the Serpent.** An open plaza ending in the colossal head with its mouth as a shelter, a camp and a boarding place, since the head faces the line.
Threat pairing: the crest is a rail, so it is also a way to be thrown, not just to ride. A rail slide hits the stair's end and leaves the rail at slide speed, off an edge or into the head block. The head at the foot is a wall at the end of a long fast slope. The headless body on the stair is a **step-sized** problem for the pilot: he sweeps the stair width (6.6 m), and the only places clear of him are the cheek crests and the head block's lee. The crest is where you flee him from, since he does not climb a rail. Breakable cheeks make a worse rail: a hole in the crest is a gap, with a broken end to be thrown off.
Catches / throws: *Standing*: stair treads are walked, and the cheeks' tops and crests are walkable (0.6 m wide, hard). *Airborne*: the crest is a rail catch (airborne contact above the rail snaps onto it: it is the balustrade slide the kit already has), the head block's top is a catch pad (8.8 x 4.4 at least, a TOP_ROOM and more), the cheek's lip is a grab. *Tumbling/stunned*: a body landing on a cheek's outer flank slides off it. A body landing *between* the cheeks on the treads tumbles down the stair (a cascade of short drops of one tier, each under the fall stun's 6 m), and arrives at the head block. A downed body on the crest slides along it (the crest is a gentle slide, SLIDE_AUTO territory), and **leaves the rail at its end as a `toss`, source `kukul_rail`**, in the rail's direction at the speed it reached (a pilot's slide speed, 6 to 9 m/s). The rail's end can be aimed by the stair's bend, so the generator can point it at the next link (a dais, a ring, a gap). *Riding*: the serpent, when it passes the stair, is a second rail beside the first, at crest height, so one can leave the stone crest and drop onto its back (the boarding).
Chains with: the serpent (the crest hands you to its back), the inhaler head (blast down the stair onto the crest), logs and the tumbling block (down the stair's middle, the crest as the dodge), the seesaw (on a landing), the headless body (swept by him, left by the crest), kukul_dais (the stair's head is the dais), kukul_ring (the crest points at it).
Breaks: cheek walls break in sections, at a drum joint about 1.9 m up for a body hit (his bulk box) and 0.9 to 1.9 m for a wave. The crest rail splits in two and falls toward the stair or away from it, as the pier does. Hit near the head block, the colossal head's jaw cracks off and the head is left leaning, its mouth a hole. Treads and landings do not break. What he breaks: the crest rail (so one rail is gone on that cheek), a place to hide (rubble beside the stair) and a new broken lip you can catch.
Build cost: **med.** The cheek serpent is a swept profile along the stair's spline (procedural, like the gothic stair and the aqueduct span) with a scale-ring pattern from the shader. The head block is one reused Blender model (a static serpent head, not rigged, around 6 to 10k tris baked). The runes are shader work: a lit-by-serpent-distance uniform, with the serpent's `rune_cut` include from the makers' glyph include (see the makers file).
Model budget: sculpt high, bake low applies to the head (about 8k tris baked, against 60k+ modelled, saving about 50k tris and an estimated 0.2 to 0.4 ms per head on screen on a mid GPU). Import LODs and visibility ranges apply to repeated cheeks (30 to 50% at range). Silhouette geometry applies to the crest chips and the feather fin (about 60 tris). Vertex-colour grime in the scale rings applies, free. Parallax depth applies to the scale rings (shallow relief, optional, 0.1 ms). Rigid parts don't apply.

# Waiting dais (kukul_dais, family kukul)
Verb: wait, hide (under the canopy), perch (a shrine and a calendar stone to read), board (step onto the serpent's back as it passes), time a crossing (the gap between the dais and the line).
Look: a great square platform, 3 cells a side (13.2 m), edge-cut at the serpent's side, where a long **trough** (a gutter 1.4 m deep and 6 m wide) is cut along the edge, as if the serpent's belly wore it there. The dais is a shrine to the line: a calendar stone (a flat round disc, thirteen day-signs and twenty glyphs in rings, the Mesoamerican count) set into the paving; a squat shrine house with a low door at its back (a place to hide and to camp). Four posts carry a canopy lintel with a feathered frieze (stone feathers, a row of day-sign blocks). Along the trough's lip run **tick stones**: eight small day-sign stelae, each lighting in turn along the line as the serpent comes, from the far end to the dais. The player *reads time* off this row. In weathered form: the canopy lintel has fallen, one post is a stump, and a root mat has heaved the paving; the shrine house is half open to the sky.
Behaviour: changes state with the serpent's lap. The tick stones light one by one as the serpent approaches (from about 10 s out, 1 per second, so there is a countdown), and the calendar stone's rune ring turns slowly (the stone is a mechanism only for show). The trough glows warm for the serpent's passing, then goes dark. **The serpent does not stop**: it pauses nothing, it paces by. The dais's edge is where the line comes within 3 m.
Size: footprint 13.2 x 13.2 m (3 CELL; over his 6x6 landing limit, so the headless body fits on it and turns). Dais top is one SET_OFF over the approach, a ledge caught from the ground with one jump. The trough is a 1.4 m deep drop, a hop down. The shrine house is 4.4 x 4.4 m, door 1.2 m high (the pilot's slide height) and 1.4 m wide, roofed by a lintel, with a back mouth (a tunnel through, as the throne has). The canopy is 4 SET_OFF (6.4 m) up; its lintel is a grab for the pump from the posts' plinths. The calendar stone is flush with the floor; its rim is a 0.2 m kerb (a cover of nothing, just a reading place). The line passes 3 to 4 m from the edge, close enough that the serpent's back (2 m above the trough floor at its crest) is within a jump of the dais lip: the boarding is a **running jump onto a moving platform** at its speed (12 m/s relative to the dais; the pilot's top speed is 8 to 9 m/s).
Collision: the paving is a set of clean walk boxes; the trough is a stepped box (a walk floor 1.4 m below, with a lip). The shrine is walls plus a tunnel (a walked floor inside plumb walls and a roof hull). The canopy posts are plumb hulls (kickable, with the lintel as a grab). The tick stones are small plumb boxes. The calendar stone is a flat disc of clean boxes.
Motifs it enables:
- **The Station.** A dais on a ledge over the abyss, serpent line a stone's throw out. Wait in the shrine, read the ticks, run and jump on as it comes. Miss, and you wait a lap.
- **The Calendar Court.** Several daises in a ring around a pillar, each a different day-sign, with the line passing all of them: wait at one, board, get off at the next (the stop that suits you).
- **The Quiet Dais.** A dais on a far isle, the line passing it at a long distance, reached by a glide; a camp place a long way off.
Threat pairing: the dais is the safest place on the layer, and the headless body knows it: it is a landing, 13.2 m square, wide enough that he enters it, and he climbs in when you wait. Waiting is only meaty if the dais is also **where something happens to you**: the headless body arriving from the stair, or the inhaler head's round drifting past; the shrine door is a cover from the inhaler head's pull (stone blocks it completely), and the back mouth is a way out when he comes in the front. The trough is a trap: a downed body slides into it, and out of it the line is a step away or too far. A body that falls into the trough while the serpent is passing is carried a moment on its flank.
Catches / throws: *Standing*: the paving and ticks are walked. *Airborne*: the dais is a catch pad at least 8.8 x 8.8 (sized for a throw from the stair crest of kukul_balustrade, or a seesaw), and the canopy lintel a grab. *Tumbling/stunned*: a body that lands on the paving is on a flat floor (a dais is flat, an anchor not a slide); a body that lands in the trough is held in a gutter 6 m wide and slides the trough's length, then is **deposited at the trough's end** (a stop block). *Riding*: the dais is where one *meets* the serpent: the trough lip is within the serpent's reach; a stunned body on the lip within 3 m of the passing body is carried by a **wake shove** (`serpent_wake`, `toss`, about 6 m/s along the serpent's heading: the physics of a heavy thing going by, never damage). It throws, at its rim (the trough's far end), at the line's heading, and the motif aims it by placing the dais with the line passing on one side.
Chains with: the serpent (boarding), kukul_balustrade (the stair's head arrives on it), kukul_ring (the line leaves the dais through it), the headless body (arrives and hunts), the inhaler head (the shrine as cover), the wind blaster (a vent at the trough mouth that pushes you onto the serpent as it passes), the seesaw (on the dais, lobbing a body onto the serpent's back).
Breaks: the canopy posts (drum joints, 1.0 to 1.9 m up), then the lintel (breaks in two, falls in halves); the shrine house's lintel (it caves a roof gap and leaves the walls). The paving, the trough and the calendar stone **do not break**, so the dais stays 13.2 m square for his lane, but the *rubble* of the canopy fills 2 to 3 m of the paving with a new cover and a ramp (a fallen lintel half makes a ramp up to the shrine's roof). What he breaks makes the wait more dangerous, and the rubble is a second place to hide.
Build cost: **med-high.** The platform, trough, canopy, shrine and posts are procedural (Build.solid blocks). The calendar disc is a shader decal. The tick stones need a small script that watches the serpent's arrival (distance along its route to the dais, which the motif knows). The new thing is the **boarding contact**: the PilotMotor moving-platform rule (high, Opus). The shrine's tunnel reuses the throne's work.
Model budget: silhouette geometry (chipped posts, the lintel's feathered edge) applies, about 40 tris per post. Parallax depth applies to the calendar disc, optional, 0.1 to 0.2 ms. Vertex-colour grime in the trough and the plinth, free. Import LODs and visibility ranges apply (a dais is seen from across the city, so a box with a decal past 60 m). Sculpt/bake only if the shrine's door lintel carries a modelled relief (about 1k baked, against 15k).

# Threading ring (kukul_ring, family kukul)
Verb: fly through (as a pilot, no: swing and cross, since no plane exists), climb the ring (the ring is a ladder of stone), perch on its crown, time a crossing (the serpent passes through it), hide in the lee of its foot.
Look: a colossal ring of stone standing on its edge, 14 m clear in the bore (the serpent's tube), carved as a coiled serpent biting its own tail (kin to the makers' court ring, but built to the serpent's bore, and four times its size). Eight voussoir blocks make the ring, each carved as one coil of scales; the tail enters the mouth at the top. It stands on a plinth or hangs from a pylon; its crown has a cap block with a shrine niche. The inside face has a rune strip that wakes as the serpent threads: the gold runs round the bore from the entrance to the exit, and for a moment the whole ring blazes. The ring is a **gate**: in the haze it reads as a circle of dim gold on the far isles, a silhouette against nothing, the way a pier row against sky never is. A *half ring* (an arch with its feet in the ground, 7 m radius) is the grounded variant. Weathered, the ring has lost a voussoir or two at the lower arc (the line is clear above) and the feet are root-bound.
Behaviour: state change. The runes on the bore wake as the serpent threads; a pulse of light runs through the ring in the direction it goes. A beat after, the ring's mouth snaps (a one-shot jaw clatter, a stone grind), a reward for being nearby; nothing launches. The ring *does not move*.
Size: bore 14 m clear (radius 7: `KUKUL_BORE`, not a pilot size). The ring is 1.4 m thick radially and 2.2 m deep (along the axis). Its outer diameter is 16.8 m, and its crown is 18 m above its foot (4 times CHAIN_TOP + ...). The ring face is climbable: each voussoir is a stepped course (a ledge SET_OFF high, STAND deep) up the outside, so it is a ladder of chained grabs; the inside is plumb and kickable. A pilot on its crown has a 1.6 x 2.2 m perch (TOP_ROOM across).
Collision: the ring is eight convex segment hulls (a torus of hulls) with the bore clear. Each voussoir's outer course is a clean walk box (a step), the crown a walk box. The foot plinth has walk boxes. The inside surface is plumb to its tangent plane, so wall kick and slide against it are the ring's interior.
Motifs it enables:
- **The Gate of the Line.** A ring set on a causeway between two isles, the serpent line passing through the bore; you cross under it on foot as the serpent threads it overhead, as a train passes over a bridge.
- **The Lamp Ring.** A ring on a lone pillar over the abyss; light on it tells you the serpent is coming, and from its crown you can see the whole lap.
- **A Ring of Rings.** Three rings in a row, the line threading all of them, each a perch for a precise jump on to the next (a gallery of tall pieces for silhouettes in the haze).
Threat pairing: in the bore the serpent has no weave (weave share 0, so it holds the axis), so it cannot swerve: the ring is the one place its path is certain. The bore has three heights. A body on the crown (9 m above the axis) or in the lower arc (7 m below it) gets a close pass and nothing more. A body *dropped through the bore* from the crown lands on the passing back, about 7 m down, over the fall stun's 6 m, so it arrives stunned: the cost of a free ride. The headless body climbs the ring's outer courses and leaps from its crown at whatever passes, so the ring is where the Olmec and the serpent are most likely to meet.
Catches / throws: *Standing*: the crown, the plinth and the stepped courses are walked. *Airborne*: the crown is a catch pad (narrow, 1.6 x 2.2 m, a precise catch), the outer courses are grabs, the inside face is kickable for a jump up the bore. *Tumbling/stunned*: a body that falls into the bore (from the crown, from a throw) falls through the 14 m hole and lands on the serpent's back, if it is passing, or goes on down. A body that falls from the crown while the serpent passes below **lands on the back as a `toss` carried along it** (source `kukul_ring`: the drop's energy kept, not restunned). *Riding*: the ring is a **port**: a rider is carried through its bore, past the plinth, and out the far side at the serpent's pace (12 m/s). It throws along the line, unaimable except by placing the ring, but the line's *bend* leaving the ring is the motif's choice.
Chains with: the serpent (threads it), kukul_dais (the line leaves the dais through it), kukul_coil (the line enters the coil through it), the seesaw (a seesaw on the plinth lobs a body to the crown), the inhaler head (its blast onto the crown, or its pull through the bore), the headless body (climbs the ring's courses, leaps from the plinth to the crown), the wind blaster (pushing a body up the bore).
Breaks: the ring is a stack of voussoirs, and the destruction round's drum-joint logic applies in an arc: a shockwave at the plinth shakes two or three voussoirs from the lower arc free (they fall as boxes and make a rubble ramp at the foot); a body hit at the crown can loose the cap block, which falls through the bore as a 3 m block, **onto the serpent's back or the walkway beneath it**. The ring cannot fall as a whole (it's a hull torus, unbroken except for the two free pieces, so the line stays clear: the bore remains 14 m). The ring's keystone falling is the set piece: a big fall for the camera's jolt, a long dust plume in haze. Once broken, the voussoir gap makes a window you can run through at the arc's foot, and the broken ends are grabs. Because the ring is the clearance, **breaking never closes the bore**; it only opens it.
Build cost: **high.** The ring is eight reused voussoir segments (the kit already has voussoirs as a trap: scripts/traps/ voussoirs) in a procedural torus, with the biting head cut on one segment. The courses up the outside are procedural. The rune strip is shader work (the lit-by-serpent-distance uniform). The cap block's fall is a physics body (a Jolt box, 3 m) with a kill-plane. A collision torus of 8 hulls is the same method as the makers' court ring.
Model budget: silhouette geometry applies to the biting head on one segment (about 120 tris), the chipped voussoir edges (about 30 per voussoir). Sculpt/bake only if the scale-ring relief is modelled (the segment is baked at about 1.5k tris against 20k). Import LODs and visibility ranges apply strongly, since rings are the far-silhouette pieces: a ring past 80 m is a torus of 40 tris with a baked rune texture and the live emissive strip. Parallax depth applies to the scale rings, optional. Vertex-colour grime on the inside face, free.

# Coil pillar (kukul_coil, family kukul)
Verb: climb (walk a wide helical ramp up a colossal column), ride (the serpent's line spirals up the pillar: riding is climbing), jump between turns (the coil's whorls are stacked ledges), hide (the lee of the pillar from the line), wall kick (the pillar's shaft from turn to turn).
Look: a colossal pillar, 17.6 m across (4 CELL), a stone column the width of a tower, with a **helical ledge cut round it** like the coil of the serpent that wound up it once; the ledge is a broad ramp, its outer edge a low parapet carved as a serpent's flank (scale rings, one rune band in three), its head at the top as a cap with a serpent's face looking out. The pillar is the isle: an upright stone in the abyss with its foot lost in the haze. Between turns, the shaft is plain basalt with a frieze of day-signs, one for each turn. Roots and the root mat grip the lowest turns. At the top, the cap is a platform with a shrine niche. It is the city's tallest thing and its most obvious silhouette; a pillar that is a stair. Weathered, part of the parapet is gone, a piece of the ramp's outer lip has broken away, and the serpent's cap-face has lost its jaw.
Behaviour: static, but it is the serpent's track. The serpent's line winds round the pillar one turn at a time: the line sits 9 m outside the shaft (shaft radius 8.8, so axis radius is 18 m, bore 7 m), and rises with the ramp, so riding it is a **climb of about 8 m a turn**. The serpent lights each turn's rune band as it passes (a spiral of gold climbing the pillar).
Size: ramp **8.8 m wide** (2 CELL; his stair limit is 5 m) with a pitch of about 14 degrees (walkable, the floor angle is steeper, and a gentle one for him), and a turn rises 8 SET_OFF (12.8 m) per whorl if it is a walked ramp, or 5 SET_OFF (8 m) per whorl if the pitch is steeper. The whorls are stacked directly above one another with a **clear gap of 6.4 m (4 SET_OFF)** between a ramp and the underside of the next (headroom, not a crawl), so the shaft between whorls is a fall of 6.4 m, over the fall stun's 6 m (a drop is a stun). The parapet is 0.8 m high, at the pilot's rail height: the pilot rail-slides along it. The cap is 13.2 x 13.2 m at least. One coil is 2 to 5 whorls. The foot is lost in the haze, so the pillar is seen as a shaft of unknown length.
Collision: the ramp is a run of clean sloped boxes along the helix (each 2.2 m long segment is a box rotated to the pitch, with walkable tops under the floor angle). The parapet is a chain of small sloped boxes (a rail). The shaft is a stack of cylinder hulls (a wall kick and a plumb). The cap has walk boxes.
Motifs it enables:
- **The Spiral Climb.** The pillar as a stair to the sky: you walk the ramp, or ride the serpent up the outside, and it brings you to the next layer's door. The line goes around; the choice is one of speed against waiting.
- **The Inside Track.** A pillar with a ramp and a stair, and a ring at the top: the serpent coils *once* and leaves through a ring.
- **Pillar Pair.** Two coil pillars wound opposite ways, joined by a ring the line threads, with a rope bridge or a dais between at the same turn (to hop between the two climbs).
Threat pairing: the ramp is wide enough that he runs up it: **the headless body climbs it as a stair** (the helix is a ramp wider than 5 m and shallow, which is his best terrain), and he can leap from turn to turn across the shaft's 6.4 m gap when he sees you on the turn above (his ledge-to-ledge leap). The danger is *the drop*: a hit on the ramp's outer edge throws you off the pillar, and the fall is long. The serpent's line hugs the outside, so the pilot on the parapet is within a body's width of its flank as it passes: **a rail slide ends at the serpent's coil**.
Catches / throws: *Standing*: the ramp is walked, and the cap is a perch. *Airborne*: each whorl's ramp is a catch (8.8 m wide), the parapet a rail (airborne contact snaps), the shaft is kickable. *Tumbling/stunned*: a body on the ramp **slides down the ramp's pitch** only if the ramp is steeper than the floor angle; at its default 14 degrees it is walked, and a stunned body stays where it landed. A body that slides off the parapet falls the whorl's height to the next ramp (6.4 m). A body knocked inward goes into the shaft (a plumb wall) and drops to the ramp. *Riding*: the coil is the family's **lift**. A body on the serpent's back goes up 8 m a turn at 12 m/s along the helix (a lap of about 18 m radius is 113 m a turn, so about 9 s a turn). Getting off is a jump to the ramp (the line is 9 m outside the shaft; the ramp's outer parapet is 2 to 3 m from the body at the moment of passing). The coil throws, off the cap or any whorl's outer edge, along the tangent (the serpent's heading), source `kukul_coil`, with a **cap fling**: at the top, where the serpent leaves the pillar, a rider who stays aboard continues on the line; one who jumps off at the end is tossed at 12 m/s along the tangent, which is the longest jump in the kit.
Chains with: the serpent (the coil is its route), kukul_ring (enters and leaves through one), kukul_dais (the pillar's foot or the cap is one), the headless body (climbs it as a stair, leaps its gaps), the inhaler head (a round around the shaft, whose blast throws you onto the serpent), the seesaw (on the cap), the wind blaster (the shaft's mouth blows a body up the outside).
Breaks: the parapet breaks in sections (the same drum-joint rule), and a **ramp's outer lip** cracks away under his landing shockwave: a bite 1.8 m wide out of the ramp's edge for 8 to 12 m of its length. That leaves the ramp **7 m wide** (8.8 less 1.8), which is still over the 6 m limit; no coil bite is ever deeper than 1.8 m. The cap-face's jaw falls from the top and lands on the whorl below, a block the size of a pilot-and-a-half, and rolls down the ramp as a tumble block (the existing tumble trap's physics). Nothing the pillar does makes it fall: the helix is the pillar's own stone, not a stack. What he breaks is the rail, the lip's edge and the face; the climb stays.
Build cost: **med-high.** The shaft is a procedural cylinder (a lathe), the helix ramp is a swept procedural mesh with a clean-box collision set along the curve (the same method as the grand stair and its landings). The cap-face is one reused Blender model (the serpent's head again, a variant of kukul_balustrade's, sharing the model). Day-sign frieze is a shader decal. The lit spiral is the uniform-by-distance strip. The tumble block is existing physics.
Model budget: sculpt high, bake low applies to the cap-face only (shared with the balustrade's head). Silhouette geometry applies to the parapet's chips and the shaft's broken lip, about 100 tris per whorl. Import LODs and visibility ranges apply hardest here: the pillar is the longest sight-line in the city, so a whorl past 60 m becomes a lathe with a baked normal and an emissive strip (a saving of around 70% of the whorl's draw). Merging whorls into one draw (RuinKit `merge`) cuts a 5-whorl pillar from about 10 draws to 2. Parallax depth applies to the frieze, optional. Vertex-colour grime is free.

# Rune collar (kukul_collar, family kukul)
Verb: ride (the stone saddle: handholds on the serpent's back), hang on (a ledge hang on the collar's lip), perch (a lantern cage on its spine), hide (the cage's lee), get thrown (the collar's lantern is where the serpent shrugs you).
Look: the city's **jewellery for the serpent**: stone and gold bands that the makers fitted round its body, as a rider's harness. A **collar** is a wide ring of carved stone, 1.2 m deep, fastened round the body at ring 30 and 60, with a lantern cage of stone lattice standing on the spine, 2.2 m high, in which a ember burns (a cut-jade lantern of hot amber). The collar's top is a flat saddle, wide enough to stand; its rim has a carved handle that a hanging pilot can grab. It reads, in the haze, as a **moving lamp**, two warm points drifting across the dark with the serpent's long pale runes between them, so the serpent is a string of lights even where its body is lost. On the plain serpent (rune-serpent.md) the runes are warm gold already; the collars add *carryable* light, and a place to stand. A rider who stands on the saddle is a few metres higher than the body's back (2 m radius body, 1.2 m collar, 2.2 m cage), a silhouette in the gold. Unfitted, the serpent has no harness; fitted serpents belong to the **processional lap** (a motif switch).
Behaviour: moves with the serpent. The collar is rigid on its ring; the cage's ember pulses slowly with the serpent's rune pulse. When the serpent weaves, the collar sways ±3.3 m laterally over a 46 m wave (a rider feels a slow, long sway). When the serpent dives, the collar tilts; a rider keeps footing while its pitch is under the floor angle, and slides down the back when it is over. The collar is the **only** kit piece that is physically attached to the serpent.
Size: collar saddle 2.4 x 2.4 m on top (TOP_ROOM and more, a stand with room to crouch, at least STAND on each side), with the cage's base 1.4 m across at its centre; the saddle is the pilot's standing height above the back (SET_OFF, 1.6 m, above the crest: the first grab for a boarder, caught by one jump). The lantern cage is 2.2 m high, a lattice the pilot can climb (the cage's bars are ledges every SET_OFF), and its top is a perch. The handle rim is at CHAIN_TOP from the back (a pump catches it). Two collars to a serpent at minimum, at ring 30 (near the head) and ring 60.
Collision: the collar is a set of clean boxes riding the capsule transforms (it follows the serpent's physics frame, like its capsules, at the 13-capsule rate). The saddle top is a walk box. The cage is plumb hulls with ledges. The handle is a small grab-edge box.
Motifs it enables:
- **The Procession.** The serpent fitted with two collars, in a lap that visits daises and rings; the collars are what you stand on and what lights your way. A mobile place.
- **The Night Lap.** A layer with no other light but the collars' amber and the serpent's runes; the player steers between pillars by its string of lamps.
- **The Rider's Duel.** The headless body leaps from a pillar's ramp onto the collar (his leap-grab), and the pilot must shake him or drop him off a ring.
Threat pairing: the collar is where **the Olmec and the serpent meet.** The body's leap-grab (whole-body-leap-grab-round2.md) catches a hanging ledge; the collar's handle is a ledge that moves at 12 m/s. If he leaps from a coil's ramp or a ring's crown as the serpent passes, he can land on the saddle, and then **he is carried**: a headless body of his weight riding the back of the serpent, hunting the pilot there. His weight shifts the collar? No: the serpent has no physics response; he is a ride-along. His landing shockwave (his pounce landing) on the collar sends a wave out along the saddle: the rider is knocked, and the shock is aimed *along the body*. The danger of standing on a collar is that it is the best seat in the city, and he knows it. And the serpent's own habit of noticing: it turns its head to **look at what it carries** (it notices the pilot within 120 m) and cracks its jaw: it does not shake you off, but it does *watch you*, and that tells you he is aboard.
Catches / throws: *Standing*: the saddle is walked, with inherited velocity (the moving-platform rule). *Airborne*: the saddle is a catch pad sized for a jump from a dais at the closing speed (12 m/s relative), 2.4 x 2.4 m, so it is narrower than any other pad; the handle is a pump-grab; the cage bars are grabs. *Tumbling/stunned*: a stunned body on the saddle stays on it (a collar's rim is a 0.3 m kerb) and is carried; one that lands on the plain back slides down the back to the belly side and falls. The kerb is why a collar is *where you want to be knocked onto*. *Riding*: the collar accepts a rider and **throws at the dismount**: a rider who jumps off at a dais, a ring crown or a coil's cap takes the serpent's velocity as a `toss`, source `kukul_saddle`, at 8.5 to 17 m/s along the heading (the serpent's pace range) with an upward component of a pump, still stunned if the rider was. The throw is as aimed as the line: wherever the line ends, that is where you land.
Chains with: the serpent (it is part of it), kukul_dais (boarding), kukul_ring (the crown above it is a drop onto the collar), kukul_coil (carried up it), the headless body (leaps onto it), the inhaler head (blasts a rider off or onto it), the seesaw (on a dais, lobbing a body at the collar).
Breaks: the lantern cage breaks (its lattice splits like a pier into three pieces) if the body lands on the collar or a shockwave passes: the cage's lamp falls as a 0.6 m ember onto the back and out into the dark (a falling light, a rare and beautiful thing), and rubble cannot stay on a moving serpent, so the pieces fall off and drop to whatever is below (the next isle, the abyss). The collar's ring does not break; it is the serpent's. A fitted serpent that loses both cages goes dark, and the layer is dim. **Breaking the lamps is the only way to put the serpent's light out.** The lantern's replacement is not a thing: the layer stays darker.
Build cost: **high.** The collar rides the serpent's capsules (a transform follow, with the capsule frame rate), needs the PilotMotor moving-platform rule (Opus), and the kit's pieces need to be added in RuneSerpent's setup as optional attachments (small changes to scripts/rune_serpent.gd, which today has no attachments). The collar and cage are procedural (a lattice of boxes). The lamp is an emissive material plus one OmniLight (at most one dynamic light for both lamps). The falling cage uses Jolt bodies.
Model budget: silhouette geometry applies to the cage's lattice (an open lattice of 12 bars is 200 tris against a filled drum of 2k, which is the lantern's whole look). Rigid parts apply as well: the collar is a rigid mesh parented to a capsule frame rather than skinned. Vertex-colour grime in the collar's crevices, free. Import LODs: the collar is a small mesh, so no; the visibility range is the serpent's own. Sculpt/bake and parallax don't apply.

## Motif hunches the pieces would make

**The Procession Stair (motif hunch).** A grand stair (kukul_balustrade) climbs a pillar's flank to a dais (kukul_dais) at the foot of a coil (kukul_coil); the line leaves through a ring at the cap. Played as a place, it is the city's one thoroughfare, seen whole as you arrive: a gold string winding up the pillar, a stair with serpent rails, a ring burning above. Nothing is scripted: the headless body hunts through it and the serpent runs its lap, and where they meet, they meet. The dais is the *wait*, and the shrine is a place to be in while it comes.

**The Meeting (a hunch about the Olmec and the serpent).** The most interesting meeting is the one the city never made: he leaps for the collar's handle from a ring's crown, the serpent turns its head to look at what has boarded it, and neither can hurt the other (it has no damage; he has no health problem with it). The pilot is the only thing with something to lose. This is a place for the combo lab rather than a card: leave a ring and a dais within a leap's reach of each other, send the serpent through, and see how often the body lands on the saddle.
