# The Felling Road
Lenses: verb SOLVE (lure him), scale COLOSSAL, shape A LINE
Pitch: Past the temple's prow four big isles climb away in a line, and every join is shut to the pilot until the Olmec's weight opens it: a column he walks through falls into a bridge, his drop into a sunk court hops the slabs that launch you up, his landing topples a row of piers into steps, and a small island he lands on leans a lip into reach. He hunts you the whole way up, so each break is a place you walk him to, standing where he must pass.
Place: The colonnade spine as it is, then west of the temple prow (spine frame x 0, the cella at x 14..40) a line of four new isles bearing west then north-west (so it swings off the bell spires' side; see Fit), each higher: A (the Drum Court), B (the Well of Piers), T (the leaning stone, a small isle) and D (the Crown, a turf meadow). On arrival (the temple, once the head is on) you see the prow's lone drum column on its stepped plinth, a 12 m gulf, and the first isle's slab court beyond. Higher isles show over each other like a stair of islands. Ruin style throughout: no runes or frets, the ruin kit's stone, ivy and moss on the drums; city shapes (drum column, drum floor, lanes) kept.
Heights (y over the spine's TOP, podium P = +18): prow +18, A court +18 and shelf +24, B rim +25.5, B well floor +21.5 and B top +29.5, T +30 (neutral), D +36. About 18 m over the podium in all. Pilot terms: grab ~1.9 m, chain 2.6 m, jump 1.5 m (pumped); the Olmec: head height 3.36 m, stride 2.4, jumps 10 m across / 1.7 up, drops to 5 m, climbs to ~44 m.

Play (the Olmec's head goes on in the cella; he is whole, hunting, and the temple gate is behind you):
1. The prow (felling). The gulf west of the altar is 12 m, over his jump (10 m) and your jump. The one way over is the column: a drum stack on a stepped plinth at the brink, its fall axis pointing across the gap. He comes through the cella and straight at you; the column stands on the neck of the prow (the prow is a 10 m neck, so he cannot skirt it; the planner reads standing pieces as air and breaks them). Stand at the brink beside the column, off its fall line by a body width. He walks into it, it falls away from his hit across the gulf (2.4 s, slow first), you are not under it (the shaft knocks you out from under it). The drums come to rest on A's rim as a 5.5 m round bridge, its crown 5.5 m over the rim. Use the plinth's steps (about 0.6 m treads under a grab) and the crown, run the drum. Bridge two ways: he follows over it.
2. A, the Drum Court. At the bridge's far end the drop is 5.5 m (under the 6 m fall stun; hang off the drum end and drop 3.5 m to be gentle). Below is a 20 x 20 m court of loose slabs on hollow drums. Run to the north half and stand on a slab. He walks the bridge, drops off its end onto the court (a leap landing, power 1): the slabs hop outward in a ring. Jump 0.24 s before the front reaches your slab: you are launched 4.4 to 5.6 m up, a grab over A's north wall (6 m), onto the shelf. Mistime it and you are knocked 3 m up and out (stunned, not hurt). He climbs the wall after you (a haul, a smaller ring, hopping the slabs again).
3. The shelf to B (floating stones). A string of floating stones, 9 m of gap in all (inside his jump and past yours without them), crosses to B's rim, 1.5 m up. He leaps it.
4. B, the Well of Piers. B's middle is a well sunk 4 m (the rim to the floor is a drop for him, a stair down the near side for you). Against its far wall's foot stand five of the hall's colossus columns in a row; above them the wall is 8 m, its first 4.5 m flat and smooth (over your chain even from a jump), its upper half cut into set-offs 1.6 m apart (a ledge chain). Go down the stair and stand on the well's flank, off the row. He drops in after you (a 4 m leap, his landing): the wave topples the piers within 0.62 of its reach away from the landing, into the wall, and they burst into drums that heap at its foot to about 2 m (check). Stand on the heap (2 m) and chain up: heap + 2.6 m chain reaches the first set-off. Climb the chain. He climbs the face after you.
5. T, the leaning stone. A 28 m flat stone floats 7 m past B's top (inside his jump). D's lip is 4.5 m over T's far edge, over a chain. Stand on T's far edge. He leaps onto T's near edge (landing power 1): the stone tips toward him (9 degrees most, over about 14 m radius that raises the far edge about 2.4 m for under a second), the far edge lifts into a chain's reach of the lip (4.5 - 2.4 = 2.1 m). You are carried and lurched down the slope (3.1 m/s, slower than running); jump at the kick's peak, grab the lip, and go over. Late, and you slide to his feet (his swat). While he stands out on T it holds a 4 degree lean (1 m): not enough alone.
6. D, the Crown. A wide meadow, ruin gate and wind horn at its head. The chase ends here: you step through the gate into the hollow under its cornice (a ruin version of the city lip's undercut hide; out of his reach, sight and inhale), he loses you, seeks, and walks back. Or run the meadow's 26 m run and take off if you came by plane to D.
Choices: stand on the prow's brink near the fall line (the bridge falls sooner, you are nearer him) or far (he veers); on the slab court, which slab (the further from the bridge's foot, the later the ring but the shorter his swat); on B, whether to crowd the row (it falls on you) or leave a wide berth (it falls thin and the heap is lower); on T, whether to hop at the kick or wait for him to walk out (lean, no kick).

Threat: The Olmec himself, whole and hunting (chase on, swat at catch reach, a pounce 10 m across, the inhale), with the fall stun and his shockwaves as the ground-level hits. Each lure is also the danger: the column's shaft, the court's knock, the piers' fall, T's lurch. Reaching the next join is an arrival, not a safe place: each isle lets him climb after you.

Ports: Catches the spine's temple prow (the pilot on the podium) and the plane at the south bench or D. Throws out nothing; D is the end. A pilot who flies to D is safe at first, since L1 holds him until you come back and lure him.
