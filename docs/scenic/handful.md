# Handfuls

**You release something; the world decides what becomes of it.** A handful is not a paintbrush. (One exception since 2026-10-02: tools that put new life in, flowers first, paint it directly; the wind and the world spreading it are kept for life that already grows. See "Flower seed".) Where it goes and what it becomes come from the land and the air: the shape of the ground decides how water fills, and the wind and the ground decide where pollen settles and what grows. The player does not decide the outcome by where they paint. The user, on 2026-09-27, after trying a seed sprinkle: "I just don't like it as a paintbrush... the geography and the environment impacts what gets grown. In the case of the water ball, the shape of the geometry affects the water, how it fills. Similarly the seeds need to be affected by the wind, and if there's no wind, it just kind of floats down."

Agreed 2026-09-27 (branch `feature/handful-shadow`), after the user said the water ball "tends to put water in the wrong spot, not where you release it" and had not noticed its shadow at all. This is the vocabulary for tools that carry something and let it go, and the one rule they keep.

## Words

- **Handful.** The thing a tool carries in the hand and lets go: water (the water ball, `scripts/water_ball.gd`), pollen for forest (`scripts/pollen_cloud.gd`, `docs/scenic/brushes.md` "Pollen"), and later pollen from flowers and pollinators (flowers themselves are painted, below, "Flower seed"). A handful has a size that grows while it is gathered, and a place on the ground where it will land.
- **Seed.** Since 2026-09-28 forest is thrown by hand: each throw flies with real momentum and lands where the physics and the wind take it, and a soft footprint shows where the next throw will come down (`docs/scenic/brushes.md` "Thrown seed"). The veil described next is behind `--pollen-veil`.
- **Pollen (the veil, `--pollen-veil`).** Forest is a veil of pollen and seed-fluff, light enough that the air carries it. While the button is held, a sweep lays the veil in the air along the pointer's way, and nothing lands. On release the whole veil comes down together: in still air straight down in the swept shape, in wind smeared and drifted downwind. Three earlier tries were turned down on 2026-09-27: a gathered handful of winged seeds (hold, then release), a seed sprinkle that poured while the button was down (it was "a paintbrush"), and a single released cloud that a drag only moved (the user found letting it all go at the last point unintuitive). In the user's words, what they want instead: "you kind of drag around and then you release a bunch of pollen that starts in the air, and then that area that you dragged descends from the air, and then the wind kind of arranges it."
- **Shaping tools** work the ground directly under the pointer while the button is down: land and path. They stay brushes (`docs/scenic/brushes.md`).
- **Sowing tools** release a handful onto the land: water falls, pollen drifts. What they make is decided by where it lands and what the land does with it, never by a painted outline.
- **The lifecycle**, in order:
  - *gather*: the press, and the handful growing while held (the ball condensing and filling);
  - *carry*: following the pointer, a little behind it, alive (the ball's spring, bob and wobble);
  - *release*: letting go (the water ball is tossed up and popped in the air, `docs/scenic/god-hand.md`);
  - *fall* (or *drift*, for light things like pollen): straight down from where it was let go;
  - *land*: the impact, on the ground under the handful (the splash);
  - *take*: the land accepts it (the pour settles);
  - *grow*: what it becomes (a pond, a stream, a waterfall; later a meadow).

  Pollen is *laid* rather than gathered: the sweep is its carry, and the veil is the handful. At release it takes a breath (lifts and gathers in) and then *drifts* rather than falls: it sinks as one sheet, peeling down along the sweep, each mote on the air at its own pace, and the wind carries it all the way down. It *takes* where the ground suits it (moisture, water, slope, height, rock, snow, existing forest) and *grows* into a stand of the species the ground's stand holds.

## The shadow is the promise

Every handful casts a **mark** on the ground straight below it, from the moment it is gathered until it lands. The mark shows exactly where it will land, and what lands must land inside the mark.

What this guarantees:

- The mark sits straight below the handful's own x, z, not under the pointer and not along the sun. The handful may trail the pointer; the mark moves with the handful.
- Release changes nothing about where. The handful falls straight down: whatever sideways speed it carried goes into its wobble, not its path. The x, z where it lands equals the mark's centre at the moment of release.
- The landing height is the terrain under the handful, not under the pointer (`WaterBall.ground_at`), so the fall ends on the ground the mark lies on.
- The handful never swings out past the land's edge, so the mark is always on ground it can land on.
- The mark is noticeable on bright and dark ground, by day and at night, and it is the only shape under the handful that could be taken for a landing spot. No sun-cast shadow competes with it.

What it does not promise: where the land takes the handful once it has landed. Water runs downhill to the basin it drains to (`docs/scenic/water-brush.md`), and seeds will root where the ground suits them. That run should read as the handful being taken, not as it teleporting; for water it does not yet (see the water brush's known gaps).

For pollen, which drifts, the promise is a footprint: a soft pool on the ground that follows the swept shape while the veil is laid, showing where it will settle given the wind as it blows now. In still air it lies right under the sweep. In wind each part of it is shifted and stretched downwind. The wind is read every frame at several places along the veil, so a gust through the falling veil carries the part it passes, and the footprint moves with it, as the ball's mark moves with the ball. Every mote lands inside the footprint as it stands at that moment. In steady air that is the footprint shown at release. The footprint is drawn in the water ball's family, softer and lighter, with a warm rim and faint twinkling specks (`shaders/pollen_footprint.gdshader`). Since 2026-09-29 it is drawn only with `--show-promise` (`PollenCloud.show_prints`), because the user found the outlines too many and too hand-holding. The veil drifting in the air and the sprouts where it lands show where it went.

## The water ball's mark

`shaders/water_ball_ground.gdshader`, the ball's ground decal. The mark is a pooled shadow the size of the ball (`radius`), drawn as a slowly turning `MARK_SIDES`-gon (12, turning `MARK_TURN` 0.12 rad/s) so it is faceted like the ground. It is painted in two tones, a darker core inside 0.58 of the radius and a lighter band out to the edge. A pale rim of water light sits on the edge, lit by the sky as well as the sun with a small floor that rises at night, so it reads on dark ground. The rim and size breathe with the ball's bob (`breath`, ±3.5 %). Held high (`MARK_HIGH`, 2 m of gap or more) the mark is soft and lighter (0.3 + 0.08 dimming, edge 0.16 of the radius); as the ball falls it darkens (0.44 + 0.14) and its edge sharpens (0.05). Sunlight gathered by the ball plays as caustics inside it by day. The mark shows from the ball's first moment (`mark` = the hold ramp × smoothstep(0.05, 0.3, radius)) and stays through the fall until the splash takes over.

## Flower seed

**Flowers are painted** (since 2026-10-02, a second pass the same day). The first pass sowed flowers as trees are sown: a veil of seed flicked from the pinch that the breeze carried 5 to 10 m downwind and that bloomed 6 to 10 s later where it landed. The user tried it and turned it down: "The pollen is too frustrating to use. When you're using the tool, it should be more like a paintbrush. I should be able to paint flowers. Once the vegetation exists, then the pollen makes sense — bend back the tree for pollen, maybe the flowers can release pollen, eventually butterflies and bees spread it around. It's just too much work to get it how you want it, and people are going to wrestle with that."

So the rule at the top of this page has an exception: **a tool that puts life in paints it directly; the wind and the world spreading it are for life that already exists.** The first of that is the dandelion's round (docs/scenic/dandelions.md): seed clocks shed on the breeze and the wind tool's gusts, come round through gold, and a few of their seeds take near the flowers already standing. The veil (`scripts/pollen_cloud.gd` with a flower look, `NativeChunkEditor._flower_press`, `_write_flowers`, `FlowerSowing.grow`) is kept, unused by the slots, for pollen released later by plants that are there (a bent tree, flowers) and by pollinators. Tree seeds (oak, pine, willow), reeds and stones are unchanged.

**How the player does it.** Press a flower in the bar's seed group (Wildflower, Dandelion, Poppy, Cosmos, Sunflower). It rides as a pinch of seed under the pointer, and a faint stitched ring with a faint fill lies on the ground under it: the brush, as wide as it paints (the Modeller's mark, `scripts/ground_marks.gd` kind `flower`; it stays, fainter, while the press paints). Press on the land and drag: flowers come up under the brush as it goes, growing in at once. Go over them again, or hold the brush still a moment, and the drift packs toward a full core; its edges feather. A quick tap paints a small dab. The roll's pips (or `1` `2` `3`) size the brush (1.0, 1.6 and 2.4 m across the radius). **Shift-drag thins** the flowers under the brush: one pass takes some, going over it clears it. Cmd+Z takes back one stroke. Esc, the Hand or the slot again puts the flower down.

**Why Shift thins.** The scheme's Shift is "fine fingers", the careful version of a verb, and two conventions already speak to it here: the Brush's Shift wipes its paint off whatever the mode, and Shift on a plant plucks it out whatever is in hand. A flower brush is a Brush that lays flowers, so its Shift takes them off, under the same footprint. Without it the only way to take painted flowers back would be undo or plucking them one at a time.

**The world still decides where.**

- *The ground.* A flower only stands on open ground: not on water (live water 2 cm deep or more, a pond, the blend's wet ground), not on rock (steeper than the habitat's rock slope, or cells painted at least half rock, earth or sand), not within 1.2 m of the snowline, not steeper than 0.75, not under a tree's crown or where three trees stand within 2.6 m, and not on a stone or another plant (`FlowerSowing.suits`, `_free`). Painted over such ground, the brush simply leaves it bare.
- *The composition* is the meadow types' (scripts/meadow_types.gd), as before: the brush's coat stands in for a drift's presence, so a painted core is packed (heads nearly touching, as at a generated drift's heart), its edge thins and feathers, a few companions mix in, and sunflowers keep to rows (a stand, not a carpet). Under a dense core the ground is warmed toward the flowers' colour, faintly (`WASH` 0.16 at most, the grass leaning `WASH_LEAN` 0.4 of the way), laid once at the release; the flowers carry the colour.
- *What grows:* Wildflower paints the wildflower meadow's mix (oxeye daisy, buttercup, poppy, cosmos, lupine in drifts of one kind), or, in a world made as a meadow type, that type's own mix and drifts. Dandelion paints dandelions and seed clocks (a few buttercups), Poppy poppies (a few daisies), Cosmos cosmos in drifts of one colour (a few daisies), Sunflower a stand in rows facing the noon sun (a very few buttercups).

**How it is built.** `scripts/flower_paint.gd`, one per stroke. The brush lays a coat (FlowerSowing's seed-per-m² units, a drift starting at `THIN` 1.6 and standing full at `FULL` 7) on a 0.25 m field over the plate. Each pass lays `PASS` (5) at the brush's middle the moment it is over the ground (the most laid on a cell this visit, banked once the brush has gone past it, so coming back is another pass), full to 0.3 of the radius and easing to nothing at its rim; held still it adds `PER_SECOND` (4, a quarter of that while it moves fast); a tap lays `TAP` (11) over 0.65 of the brush. One pass comes up at about half a full drift's core, two passes or a second's linger fill it, and the cap is the drift's own packing. Each lattice spot is judged once a stroke (its draw, kind, row and ground), and only once its coat could raise it, so a painted frame only compares numbers. Shift's thinning lays the same coat; each flower goes once the coat passes its own share of the way from `THIN` to `FULL`, any flower, hand-painted or the world's, and the cell it stood in turns hand work (as a plucked plant's), so emergence does not sow it straight back.

The editor (`NativeChunkEditor.paint_flowers_begin`, `_flower_paint_process`, `paint_flowers_end`) opens one undo step at the press and closes it at the release. While the button is down there is no refresh at all: what a frame places is drawn that frame by the view's paint preview (`GardenView.paint_preview`, small batches of their own on the same meshes and materials, with the births in their custom data, so they grow in with the shader's easeOutBack rise, `SHRUB_RISE` 1.1 s, within 0.3 s of being painted), and what thinning takes is folded to nothing where it stands (`paint_hide`). The release runs the usual yielding settle once; when the land's batches publish they draw everything and the preview is cleared. The flowers are ordinary object records of the kit's keys, marked hand work (`source: "hand"`), with Emergence's `born` stamp, which is never saved. Old saves load and dress unchanged.

**Measured** (`tests/test_hand_flowers.gd`, headless; `tools/film_hand_flowers.gd`, rendered on an M5 Pro at 1280×720, vsync 60). One 12 m pass of the middle brush paints about 240 poppies, its middle about 11 a square metre and two passes 20 (the cap for a wildflower-type drift); a tap 14 cosmos; a two-pass stroke of the wide brush 800 dandelions, 530 cosmos or 195 sunflowers. Main-thread paint work a frame: 0.5 to 0.8 ms mean, 3 to 7.5 ms on the press, and up to 4.6 ms on the first frame of a wide brush (the undo snapshot and the plate's survey). Frame times while painting a 150-frame poppy stroke: on the plain, 16.7 ms median, worst 17.6 ms, the same as idle; on a wildflowers meadow world of 4,500 records, 22 to 23 ms median (the same as idle), worst 24 to 26 ms. The release costs about 4 ms that frame (wash, undo step, starting the settle). The settle itself is the shared refresh every placement pays: invisible on the plain, two or three frames of 34 to 50 ms over the next 15 frames on the 4,500-record meadow world (the batches' buffer swap, about 9 ms, and the play-geometry check over every record).

## Tree seed

**Trees are painted too** (since 2026-10-02, the same day as the flowers). The user: "I realize that I'd like to be able to set trees down just like flowers. I don't want to have to use the pollen." So the tray's tree seeds (oak, pine, willow, and birch and the rest from the command palette) are a brush: `hand_materials.gd` `paints` is true for them, and `sows` is now false for everything in the case. The veil (`NativeChunkEditor.sow_from_hand`, `_write_cloud`) stays for a bent tree's own spores.

- **A tap** plants one tree of the seed's kind where it is pressed, if the ground there takes a tree and there is room.
- **A drag** paints a wood: the flower brush's coat (`PASS`, `PER_SECOND`) over a lattice `SPACING` (2.3 m) apart, each spot jittered by `JITTER` and coming up once the coat passes its own draw. One pass leaves an open stand; going over it, or lingering, fills it toward every spot (`STAND_FULL`). The brush is `TREE_RADIUS` (3 m) at the middle pip, and the ground mark's ring is drawn that wide.
- **Shift** thins the trees and dead wood under the brush, the hand's or the world's; the cells turn hand work so emergence does not sow them straight back.
- **The kind is the seed's**, always: an oak paints oaks. The land still decides where one can stand, as emergence's arrivals do (`TreePaint.suits`): not on water or its shore, not on rock steeper than the habitat's rock slope, not near the snow or above the treeline, and not nearer than `GAP` (1.7 m) to another tree or `ROOM` (0.6 m) to a stone or shrub. Flowers give way to a tree painted over them.
- Each tree is an ordinary record, sized, tinted and given its stand's autumn as an emerged one is, marked hand work, and grows in with the trees' rise.

**How it is built.** `scripts/tree_paint.gd` extends `scripts/flower_paint.gd` (its coat, tap/drag decision and thinning) and swaps in the tree spots. `NativeChunkEditor.paint_flowers_begin` takes a tree kind as its slot and makes a `TreePaint`; everything after (the paint preview, one undo step, the settle at release) is the flowers' path. Checked headless by `tests/test_hand_trees.gd` in the cozy scene: a tap sets one oak where pressed in one undo step; a 10 m pine drag paints 5-6 pines under the brush, none nearer than `GAP`, with no refresh while the button is down; two more passes add more; Shift thins and undo brings them back; none in a pit of standing water. Not yet seen on screen: the density and the brush's feel.

## Wind

The wind tool (`docs/scenic/wind-gust.md`, `scripts/wind_gust.gd`) lets go of air rather than a handful: press and drag to blow, and release to send a gust along the swipe. Its promise is a lane on the ground, adapted from the water ball's mark, that shows where the air will roll, how wide and how far. What `release` sends is exactly what the lane showed, and the air stays inside it. A still press is a burst, and its promise is a ring.
