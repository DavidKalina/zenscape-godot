# Crater lake (take 3)

**Intent:** a broad hill with a crater in its crown, filled with water: a
lake standing above the surrounding land.

**Recipe:** `tools/landscapes/crater_lake.gd`. Paddle size 3 swell to 4.5 m
at (1,0). Chisel size 3 pit pulled down 1.3 m in the crown. Water held 3 s,
then again 3 s. 15 s of world at 4x.

**Stills:** /private/tmp/zs-landscapes/crater_lake-take2/ (pinch bowl) and
crater_lake-take3/ (wide pit). The take 1 stills were overwritten.

## Takes
1. **Chisel size 2 pit, 2 m deep.** Floor 2.61 m, rim 3.42 to 3.77 m. Holds
   13.9 m³ at 1 m deep, and the overflow ran down the near-left flank into
   a pond at the foot. Looks like a **quarry or a cistern**: sheer octagonal
   rock walls, the water a metre under the lip, no raised rim. From low
   down you can't see the water.
2. **Modeller pinch down 2 m.** A soft bowl: floor 2.46 m, rim 3.24 to 3.67
   m. 6.2 m³, 1 m deep, the overflow running down the east flank to a large
   sheet on the lowland (53 m² of water). From the camera it's a **pond on
   a hill**. Trees close over it, and from low down it disappears.
3. **Chisel size 3 pit, 1.3 m.** The pit (radius about 3.75 m) is wider than
   the swell's level part. At 4.6 m out the swell had already fallen *below*
   the pit floor (floor 3.04 m, rim 2.46 to 2.60 m). So it is a level
   terrace on a hilltop, not a basin. Two pours left a 0.16 m film over 31
   m² that drains slowly (4.5 m³ of about 20 m³ poured stays on the plate).
   Oddly, this is the shot that most *looks* like a hilltop lake, because
   the water sheet fills the flat to its edge.

## How the tools felt
- **The paddle swell's "level crown" is only level very near the middle**
  (-0.4 m at 2 m out, -2.2 m at 5 m). A pit has to stay well inside it.
  The player sees a dome, not the usable flat.
- Neither pit verb gives a **crater rim**: ground raised around a hollow.
  The Chisel gives a well, the pinch-down a dimple. A real crater lake needs
  the land *outside* the hole to rise, or the water reads as just below
  ground.
- The Chisel pit is reported as `mesa` again.
- Water can't be filled "to the brim" on purpose. Each ball is a fixed size
  (8 to 11 m³), and once the lowest rim spills, more just runs away.

## Gaps
- A **ring verb**: a crater or caldera (pinch-down with a raised lip, or a
  swell with a hollow crown). The Modeller groove's 0.12 m lip is the only
  rim-raiser, and it's too small.
- Life buries the form. In 15 s at 4x, trees cover the hill and the rim,
  so the landform is lost at the default distance.
