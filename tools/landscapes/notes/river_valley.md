# River valley (take 1)

**Intent:** a meandering river running from high ground at the head, down an
S-curve channel, into a lake, with willows and reeds on the banks.

**Recipe:** `tools/landscapes/river_valley.gd`. Paddle at size 3: swell up at
the head and the west, swell down for the lake. Paddle at size 2: sweep an S
down the plate. Modeller groove for a tributary from the west. Water poured
at the head and the tributary. Willow and oak seeds, reeds round the lake.

**Stills:** /private/tmp/zs-landscapes/river_valley/ (02-shaped,
04-overview, 05-valley-low, 06-lake).

## What came out
- Water: the head pour became a **spring with a waterfall** down the swell's
  flank, which is the prettiest thing in the build. Lake 17.5 m³ over 45 m²,
  0.74 m deep. The channel itself only held 0.4 m³: **water did not read as a
  river** along the S, so the river is a fall plus a pond.
- Life: rich and quick. Frogspawn, frogs, lily pads and reeds on the lake,
  and about 100 trees over the plate in 30 s at 4x. Spruce dominates
  everywhere, so the oak and willow sowing barely shows.
- The swell at size 3 is huge: about 5.5 m tall and nearly half the plate.
  Where the sweep ran down it, it left a **dark gash** (bare earth plus a
  cliff) rather than a valley.

## How the tools felt (scripted, so this is about results, not aiming)
- **Paddle swell vs Modeller pinch:** clearly different (a broad dome vs a
  peak). Good.
- **Paddle sweep vs Modeller groove:** both read as "a dark dirt line"; the
  sweep's 0.45 m trough with low ridges doesn't read as a valley at this
  scale. Differentiated on paper, not on screen.
- **Gap:** there is no way to say "the river goes here". Water takes the
  steepest way, and a 0.45 m trough doesn't hold it against the land's fall.
