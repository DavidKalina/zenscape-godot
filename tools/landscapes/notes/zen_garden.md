# Zen garden (two takes)

**Intent:** a karesansui. A level bed, three stones of rising size, parallel
raked lines across the bed, and a small pond in a corner. Question: is the
clay fine enough for raked lines?

**Recipe:** `tools/landscapes/zen_garden.gd` (take 2). Nine Paddle pats (size
3) over a 12 m bed, then a fast Modeller drag to rub it smooth. Rake: west
half, seven Paddle narrow sweeps (Shift, size 1) 1.5 m apart; east half, five
Modeller thumb grooves (size 1) 2.5 m apart. Stones: taps held 1.6, 0.6 and
1.0 s. Pond: a size-1 Paddle hollow and a 1.2 s water ball.
- Take 1: pats, then three Modeller palm strokes to level, then nine thumb
  grooves at size 1, 1.25 m apart.

**Stills:** /private/tmp/zs-landscapes/zen_garden/ (01-bed, 02-raked,
03-garden, 04-garden-low). Take 1 is in `zen_garden-take1/`.

## What came out
- **Levelling:** pats took the bed's spread from 0.58-0.80 m down to
  0.38-0.57 m and no further. Take 1's palms made it worse: 0.65 m spread,
  because each palm sinks 0.4 m. The fast rub did nothing (spread 0.57 to
  0.59). There is no way to get a truly level bed.
- **Raked lines, take 1:** thumb grooves 1.25 m apart merged into one
  churned, dark-earth field. The profile across them had one trough and one
  hump, not nine lines. A groove at size 1 is still 0.75 m deep and 1.6 m wide.
- **Raked lines, take 2:** both halves now show distinct parallel lines (see
  02-raked). Measured, the paddle half is a regular 1.5 m wave of about
  0.5 m amplitude, and the thumb half is a 2.5 m wave of about 0.9 m. On
  screen they read as **ploughed furrows or trenches**: 0.5-0.9 m deep, with
  grass on the ridges and dark earth or shadow in the troughs. They do not
  read as raked gravel. At this plate scale (27 m across) a rake line would
  be about 5 cm deep and 20 cm apart, and the land's points sit 0.45-0.75 m
  apart. **The clay cannot be that fine**, so this is a mesh-resolution
  limit as well as a tool one.
- **Stones:** the press time worked. 0.6 s gave scale about 0.7-1.0, and
  1.6 s gave about 1.3-1.5. They come out as rounded faceted boulders, which
  read well. But there are only three asset sizes of "rock", and a big stone
  is about 1.4 m, the same as a shrub.
- **Pond:** 2.9 m³ over 9 m², 0.6 m deep. Fine.
- **Life overran it.** In 15 s at 4x the bed grew birch, spruce, juniper,
  snags and shrubs (take 2: 31 plants on the bed against 3 rocks). The deer
  walked through. The garden's emptiness, which is the whole point of a
  karesansui, can't be kept.

## How the tools felt
- **Paddle narrow sweep at size 1:** the best "line" tool. It gives a trough
  with a ridge either side, so parallel strokes make an even ribbing, and
  it takes direction well.
- **Thumb groove:** fine for one channel. In parallel it needs at least 2.5 m
  spacing or the lips and beds merge. Its "the bed never climbs" rule digs
  progressively deeper along a stroke that crosses bumps, so lines on a
  slightly uneven bed come out uneven.
- **Palm:** reads as "dig a pan", not "smooth". A player levelling a garden
  would reach for it and get a hole.
- **Flaky presses:** in take 1 the pond hollow, pressed from a close
  camera, took no swell at all (402 px of pull). From the overview it worked.
  I couldn't pin the cause (possibly the press landing as something other
  than land). The same happened once in pastoral_downs.

## Gaps
- **No fine surface verb.** A rake (a comb drawn over the land, writing a
  tint or a normal pattern rather than height) would give raked gravel at any
  scale.
- **No sand or gravel material.** The bed is grass, and dug ground turns brown
  earth. A karesansui needs a pale ground paint.
- **No true level.** The Chisel's shelf is the only verb that writes dead
  level ground, and it adds a face downhill. Something like a Paddle "press
  level" (Shift-tap) is missing.
- **Nothing keeps ground clear.** Raked ground could deter seedlings, or a
  stone border could mean "garden".

## Scores
Buildable 2/5 · Looks 2/5 (furrowed field, not a garden) · Matched intent 1/5
